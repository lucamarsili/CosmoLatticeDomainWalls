\section{Generalised VOS equations for $\mathbb{Z}_N$ domain walls}
\label{sec:vos-zn}

The Velocity-dependent One-Scale (VOS) model~\cite{} provides an effective
description of a domain wall (DW) network in terms of two macroscopic
variables: a characteristic correlation length $\ell(t)$ and an rms
velocity $v(t)$. For the prototypical $\mathbb{Z}_2$ case the equations
read~\cite{}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vos-z2}
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{d\ell}{dt} = (1+3v^{2})\,H\ell + c_w\,v + d\,\bigl[k_0 - k_w(v)\bigr]^{r}\,, \\[1.0ex]
\dfrac{dv}{dt}    = (1-v^{2})\left(\dfrac{k_w(v)}{\ell} - 3Hv\right)\,,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the velocity-dependent momentum parameter
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kw}
k_w(v) = k_0\,\frac{1-(qv^{2})^{b}}{1+(qv^{2})^{b}}\,,
\end{equation}
and the same numerical constants used in Sec.~\ref{sec:vos-comparison}
(\,$c_w \!=\!0$, $d_2\!=\!0.26$, $r\!=\!1.42$, $q\!=\!3.35$, $b\!=\!1.08$,
$k_0\!=\!1.77$\,). The first three terms on the right of the
$\ell$-equation correspond, respectively, to Hubble stretching, particle
radiation and energy loss through wall collisions
(\emph{chopping}). The number of walls per Hubble volume is
$\mathcal{A}(t) \equiv 1/(2H\ell)$, and the well-known scaling solution
for $\mathbb{Z}_2$ in radiation domination takes the values
$\mathcal{A}_{*}\simeq 0.9$, $v_{*}\simeq 0.38$.

Going from $\mathbb{Z}_2$ to a general $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry adds two
qualitatively new ingredients to the network. First, the vacuum manifold
splits into $N$ disconnected components, so a typical wall meets a
\emph{compatible} partner with which it can pair-annihilate only a
fraction $\sim 1/(N-1)$ of the time, suppressing the energy loss into
free radiation. Second, when $N\geq 3$ the energetically preferred local
configurations are not isolated wall segments but \emph{wall junctions}
where three or more domains meet along a one-dimensional locus. These
junctions exert an additional drag on the network: a wall attached to a
junction can no longer freely shrink under its own surface tension, and
the rate at which the network loses length to chopping is reduced
accordingly. Both effects act on the source term of the
$\ell$-equation, and we parametrise them by a single $N$-dependent
coefficient $d_N$ that replaces $d_2$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vos-z2}.

We adopt the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dN}
d_N \;=\; \frac{2\,d_2}{N}\;\frac{1}{1 + \alpha_J\,(N-2)}\,,
\end{equation}
which has the following structure. The prefactor $2/N$ encodes the
combinatorial dilution of chopping channels in the network of $N$
disconnected vacua, and reduces to $d_N\!=\!d_2$ at $N=2$.\footnote{%
A multi-component VOS formalism for $\mathbb{Z}_N$ networks, in which
walls and junctions are evolved as separate energy components, was
developed in Refs.~\cite{} (Avelino, Martins, Menezes and
collaborators). Our ansatz~\eqref{eq:dN} should be viewed as a
single-component reduction of that picture: rather than tracking the
junction sector explicitly, we absorb both the combinatorial dilution
of pair-annihilation channels and the junction drag into a single
$N$-dependent coefficient. The prefactor of $2$ in $2 d_2/N$ is fixed
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN} reproduces the standard $\mathbb{Z}_2$ value
at $N=2$ exactly.} The denominator is the new ingredient introduced in this
work: it switches on only for $N\geq 3$ through the factor $(N-2)$, and
captures the suppression of chopping by junction friction. The
dimensionless parameter $\alpha_J\geq 0$ measures the strength of this
suppression per additional wall species attached to a junction.
At $N=2$, where no junctions exist, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN} reduces to
$d_N=d_2$ identically and the original VOS equations are recovered. For
$N\to\infty$, $d_N\to 0$ and the network ceases to chop, reflecting the
expectation that a network entirely made of frustrated junctions cannot
relax through pair-annihilation alone.

\paragraph{Scaling fixed point.}
The scaling solution $\ell = \xi\,t$, $v = v_{*}$ in radiation domination
($H=1/2t$) reduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:vos-z2} to the algebraic system
\begin{align}
\label{eq:fp1}
\mathcal{A}_{*} \;&=\; \frac{3\,v_{*}}{2\,k_w(v_{*})}\,, \\[0.5ex]
\label{eq:fp2}
\mathcal{A}_{*} \;&=\; \frac{1 - 3 v_{*}^{2}}
                          {2\,d_N\,\bigl[k_0 - k_w(v_{*})\bigr]^{r}}\,,
\end{align}
where Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp1} comes from $\dot v = 0$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp2} from balancing Hubble stretching against chopping in
the $\ell$-equation. Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fp1}--\eqref{eq:fp2} can be solved
for $(v_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*})$ for each $N$. Two qualitative features
are worth highlighting. First, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp1} depends only on
$v_{*}$ and is therefore $N$-independent at fixed $v_{*}$; the whole
$N$-dependence of $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ enters through Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp2}
via the source coefficient $d_N$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN}. Second,
because $k_w(v)$ is a decreasing function of $v$, larger $N$ (and hence
smaller $d_N$) drives the system to a \emph{higher} rms velocity, which
both reduces $k_w(v_*)$ on the right of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp1} -- thereby
raising $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ -- and enlarges $k_0-k_w(v_*)$ on the right of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp2}, partially compensating the suppression of $d_N$.
The two effects act coherently and yield a monotonic increase of both
$v_{*}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ with $N$.

The resulting evolution and fixed points are shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:vos-zn-evol} and Fig.~\ref{fig:vos-zn-fp}. Numerically,
with $\alpha_J=0.5$ as a representative value, the late-time plateaus
in Fig.~\ref{fig:vos-zn-evol} shift from $\mathcal{A}_{*}\simeq 1$,
$v_{*}\simeq 0.39$ at $N=2$ to $v_{*}\simeq 0.55$ for $N=20$, with
$\mathcal{A}_{*}$ still increasing on the time window of integration.
For the simpler ansatz $d_N=d_2/N$ with no junction friction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:vos-zn-fp}, used for clarity in isolating the
combinatorial dilution), we find that $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ grows as a
power law in $N$ to good accuracy,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Astar-fit}
\mathcal{A}_{*}(N) \;\simeq\; 0.79\,N^{0.60}\,,
\end{equation}
over the range $N\in[2,50]$. Switching the junction-friction term back
on steepens this growth and shifts $v_{*}$ further upward, since the
extra suppression of $d_N$ is a multiplicative factor in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp2}. The power-law growth of $\mathcal{A}_{*}(N)$ is
the central prediction of the generalised VOS equations and provides a
clean target for our lattice measurements: any $\mathbb{Z}_N$ network
that scales must do so with $\mathcal{O}(1)$--$\mathcal{O}(10)$ walls
per Hubble volume, monotonically increasing with $N$.

\paragraph{Connection with the lattice and $\beta$ dependence of
$\alpha_J$.}
The integer $N$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN} is fixed by construction: it
labels the discrete subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_N$ enforced by the scalar
potential and is the same in every simulation of a given symmetry
class. At fixed $N$, the only free parameter in $d_N$ is the
junction-friction coefficient $\alpha_J$, which we infer directly from
the late-time scaling plateau of $\mathcal{A}(t)$ in the lattice.
Given a measured pair $(\mathcal{A}_{*},v_{*})$ at fixed $N$, we solve
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp2} for the effective source coefficient,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dN-eff}
d_N^{\rm eff} \;=\; \frac{1-3v_{*}^{2}}
                         {2\,\mathcal{A}_{*}\,[k_0-k_w(v_{*})]^{r}}\,,
\end{equation}
and then invert Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alphaJ-eff}
\alpha_J^{\rm eff}(N) \;=\; \frac{1}{N-2}\!\left[\frac{2\,d_2}{N\,d_N^{\rm eff}} - 1\right]\,, \qquad (N\geq 3)\,,
\end{equation}
to read off the junction-friction coefficient implied by the
simulation. If $v_{*}$ is not separately measured, it can be
self-consistently inferred from $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fp1}, which is independent of $d_N$. The combinatorial
$2/N$ prefactor is itself an \emph{ansatz}, and is tested -- not
extracted -- by checking whether the values of $\alpha_J^{\rm eff}$
obtained from runs at $N=3,4,\dots$ are mutually consistent. A
systematic $N$-dependent residual in $\alpha_J^{\rm eff}$ at fixed
$\beta$ would indicate that the dilution prefactor itself needs to
deviate from $1/N$.

A non-trivial observation from our $\mathbb{Z}_4$ runs is that the
extracted plateau $\mathcal{A}_{*}$ -- and therefore $\alpha_J^{\rm
eff}$ -- depends appreciably on the $U(1)\to\mathbb{Z}_4$
explicit-breaking coupling $\beta$, which controls the strength of the
$\mathbb{Z}_4$-preserving operator that lifts the vacuum manifold from
a continuous circle down to four isolated minima. This is physically
expected. In the $\beta\to 0$ limit the vacuum manifold becomes an
approximately flat $S^{1}$, the would-be domain walls are broad
$U(1)$-Goldstone-like configurations of width set by the soft scale
$\sim\beta^{-1/2}\,\mu^{-1}$, and the very notion of a localised
$\mathbb{Z}_N$ junction loses its meaning: ``junctions'' become
extended string-like cores around which the phase winds, rather than
sharp drag centres. In the non-adjacent, large-$\beta$ limit, the four
$\mathbb{Z}_4$ minima are well separated, walls are thin compared to
the network correlation length, and junctions reduce to clean
codimension-two intersections of three or four walls with a definite
local energetics. Junction friction is therefore expected to be
\emph{absent} in the unbroken-$U(1)$ limit and to saturate to a finite
value in the deep $\mathbb{Z}_N$ limit,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alphaJ-beta}
\alpha_J \;=\; \alpha_J(\beta)\,, \qquad
\alpha_J(\beta\to 0)\to 0\,,\qquad
\alpha_J(\beta\to\infty) \to \alpha_J^{\rm sat}\,.
\end{equation}
Correspondingly, $d_N$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN} interpolates between
$d_N=2d_2/N$ at small $\beta$ -- where chopping is suppressed only by
the combinatorial dilution of pair-annihilation channels -- and the
fully junction-suppressed value
$d_N = (2 d_2/N)/[1+\alpha_J^{\rm sat}(N-2)]$ at large $\beta$. A
simple one-parameter parametrisation consistent with our data is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alphaJ-form}
\alpha_J(\beta) \;=\; \alpha_J^{\rm sat}\,\frac{\beta/\beta_{\star}}{1+\beta/\beta_{\star}}\,,
\end{equation}
with a single crossover scale $\beta_{\star}$ at which the wall
thickness becomes comparable to the typical inter-wall separation;
the precise functional form, the value of $\alpha_J^{\rm sat}$ and
whether $\beta_{\star}$ depends non-trivially on $N$ are extracted
directly from the $\mathcal{A}(t)$ plateaus measured in our lattice
scans (see Sec.~\ref{sec:lattice-results}). We stress that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:alphaJ-beta} is the central reason the standard
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ VOS fit cannot be applied to small-$\beta$ $\mathbb{Z}_N$
networks at face value: the effective chopping in the simulation is
\emph{not} controlled by $d_2$ but by a $\beta$-dependent $d_N$ that
only approaches the rigid $\mathbb{Z}_N$ junction-friction value once
the underlying $U(1)$ is sufficiently broken.

We stress that, although Eq.~\eqref{eq:dN} introduces only a single
extra parameter beyond the original $\mathbb{Z}_2$ VOS, the resulting
generalised system captures the two qualitative features that
distinguish $\mathbb{Z}_N$ networks from their $\mathbb{Z}_2$
counterpart: the slowing of chopping with growing $N$, and the
sensitivity of the scaling regime to how strongly the underlying
$U(1)$ is broken to $\mathbb{Z}_N$. The latter, encoded in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:alphaJ-beta}, is what allows the same VOS framework to
interpolate continuously between the small-$\beta$ regime, where the
network is closer to a $U(1)$ Goldstone configuration with
ill-defined junctions, and the deep-$\mathbb{Z}_N$ regime, where
junctions are sharp, exert their full friction, and dominate the
late-time chopping rate that ultimately controls the
gravitational-wave signal of interest.
